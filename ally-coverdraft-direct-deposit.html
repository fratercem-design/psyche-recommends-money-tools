ALLY / DIRECT DEPOSIT

Ally CoverDraft direct deposit requirements, without the guesswork.

The basic $100 path does not require direct deposit. Expanded coverage up to $250 does, and creator-platform payouts may not count.

By the Psyche Recommends editorial desk · Provider guidance reviewed September 12, 2026

CURRENT PATH

Ally says standard CoverDraft eligibility generally begins 30 days after $100 in total deposits reaches an eligible Spending Account. Expanded coverage up to $250 requires qualifying direct deposits of at least $250 for two consecutive months.

STANDARD VS. EXPANDED

Direct deposit changes the possible limit, not the basic starting path.

PathCurrent stated requirementPossible coverage
StandardAn eligible Ally Spending Account, $100 in total deposits, then 30 days in most casesUp to $100
ExpandedFirst qualify for standard coverage, then receive a qualifying direct deposit of at least $250 for two months in a rowUp to $250

Coverage is not a loan approval, a spending target, or a guarantee that every transaction will go through. Ally describes CoverDraft as a service rather than a line of credit.

Requirements can change. Check Ally’s current CoverDraft help page.

DEPOSIT TYPE

A bank transfer and a qualifying direct deposit are not the same thing.

Ally’s current deposit-agreement language defines a qualifying direct deposit as an eligible direct deposit from an employer payroll, payroll-provider service, or benefits payer such as Social Security or military pay. The name on a payout screen does not decide whether Ally classifies the deposit that way.

  • Employer payroll may qualify when it arrives through an eligible direct-deposit channel.
  • A payroll-provider deposit may qualify if Ally identifies it as eligible.
  • Government-benefit payments may qualify under Ally’s stated definition.
  • A transfer you initiate from another bank is not described as payroll direct deposit.
  • A peer-to-peer transfer is not automatically a qualifying direct deposit.
  • Only Ally can confirm how a specific deposit was coded and counted.

FOR CREATORS AND FREELANCERS

Do not assume a platform payout will count.

Twitch, YouTube, marketplace, affiliate, and freelance-platform payouts can arrive electronically without being coded as employer payroll. A client’s bank transfer may also look like direct deposit in casual conversation while failing Ally’s product definition.

Ask a narrow question: after the deposit posts, contact Ally through its official app or website and ask whether that specific deposit was treated as a qualifying direct deposit for expanded CoverDraft. Do not send account numbers or transaction screenshots to anyone who contacts you first.

Track the posted date, amount, sender description, and Ally’s eligibility notice in your own records. The eligibility notice matters more than what the sending platform calls the payout.

KEEPING EXPANDED COVERAGE

The two-month test is followed by a 45-day maintenance rule.

After expanded coverage becomes available, Ally says at least one qualifying direct deposit must arrive every 45 days to keep it. Missing that interval may reduce the CoverDraft limit to the standard amount.

  1. Confirm standard eligibility first.

    Wait for Ally’s activation notice rather than treating the calendar alone as approval.

  2. Receive the first qualifying $250 deposit.

    Record the posted date and verify how Ally classified it.

  3. Receive another qualifying $250 deposit the next month.

    The stated expanded-coverage path requires two consecutive months.

  4. Watch for Ally’s expanded-eligibility notice.

    Ally says it will notify eligible customers, who can then change their preference to a higher amount.

  5. Keep one qualifying deposit inside each 45-day window.

    Irregular creator income can make this maintenance requirement easy to miss.

COVERAGE BOUNDARY

Eligibility does not make every transaction eligible.

Ally lists debit-card purchases, automatic payments, certain outside-institution transfers, and personal or mailed Bill Pay checks among transactions CoverDraft typically covers. It excludes Zelle payments, ATM withdrawals, immediate internal transfers, and wire transfers.
